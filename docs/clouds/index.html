Cloudstexture synthesizer · 32 kHz · home slot 8

Clouds records what you patch into J1 and J2 into a short buffer and plays it back as a cloud of tiny grains: smeared, pitched, frozen, stretched or turned into spectral mist. This is Mutable Instruments' DSP, unchanged, running on the Alchemy Lab's six pots and three buttons.

Built and native-tested. Not yet flashed or heard.
An illustration, silent: the granular mode's scheduler from granular_sample_player.h redrawn in the browser. Grain rate, regular versus random seeding, grain length (lut_grain_size), pitch (lut_quantized_pitch) and where each grain starts follow the firmware's code. The waveform is a stand-in for your input.

What it does

Four ways to take a recording apart

Everything Clouds plays comes out of a recording buffer that the input keeps writing into, about one second of 16-bit stereo. POSITION says where in that second to read, SIZE how long each piece is, PITCH how far to transpose it and DENSITY how many pieces play at once. TEXTURE shapes them and BLEND mixes the result with the dry input.

FREEZE stops the recording. The buffer holds what it has, and you play that snapshot for as long as you like. A gate on J3 does the same, so a sequencer can freeze and release it.

The original Clouds used one BLEND knob for four jobs: dry/wet, stereo spread, feedback and reverb, chosen with a button. Here each of those four has its own pot. That is why this firmware has a second page.

Modes and quality

Setup P1 and P2 · both saved with the working state
Mode 1

Granular

The classic cloud: up to 32 to 57 overlapping grains (more at lower quality). DENSITY sets how many; below noon they come at a steady rate, above noon at random. TEXTURE morphs each grain's envelope and, past 3 o'clock, adds a diffuser.

Mode 2

Stretch

A pitch-shifter and time-stretcher (WSOLA): two overlapping windows aligned by a correlation search, so pitch and speed come apart without the usual flutter. TEXTURE is a low-pass left of noon, high-pass right of it.

Mode 3

Looping delay

A delay line whose loop you can freeze and scan. SIZE sets the loop length and POSITION the delay time; FEEDBACK does what it does on a tape delay. TEXTURE filters as in Stretch.

Mode 4

Spectral

A phase vocoder. The sound is taken apart as frequency bins: TEXTURE quantizes them, DENSITY sets how often the spectrum refreshes, SIZE warps it. The FFT work runs in the main loop, like on the original.

Grain counts are the firmware's own: 32 for stereo or 40 for mono, times 23/16 at 8 bits (granular_processor.cc). Changing mode or quality resets the buffer.

Jacks and CV

Two audio pairs, two gates, V/Oct and three CVs
JackSignalHow it is read
J1 · J2Audio in, left and rightCodec input. Use IN GAIN (Setup P6) if it records too hot or too quiet.
J3Freeze gateRead raw once per 1 ms audio block; high above 1.5 V, low below 0.5 V. ORs with B1.
J4Trigger: seed a grainSame reading. The edge is held one block, as upstream holds it for its ADC, so the grain lands with the CV that came with it.
J5V/Oct → pitchCalibrated volts × 12 semitones, added to the PITCH knob. Jumps over half a semitone land at once; smaller moves glide.

Installing

From the SD card, no computer needed

Download v0.1.0 · built and tested on a computer, not yet run on a module

clouds_alchemy.binSD card zip · all six

The zip holds all six firmwares and the Elements samples, laid out for the card: unzip it onto the card's top level and the .bin files land in /alchemy, elements.smp in /mi. Release notes and SHA-256 checksums.

Copy the firmware into the card's alchemy folder next to the others. Open Settings on whatever firmware is running (hold B2 + B3 for two seconds), press B1 to reach the firmware page, choose the file with P1 and fire P2: turn it below about 85 %, then past 95 %. It writes, verifies and reboots into Clouds.

Clouds keeps its knobs in preset slot 8 (counting from 0) and saves five seconds after the last change, with your hands off the buttons. Switching to another firmware and back brings Clouds' settings back; the pots catch before they take over. Slots 0–7 are left for presets you save by hand.

build it yourself:
  make libdaisy
  make FW=clouds  →  build-clouds/clouds_alchemy.bin

v0.1 uses the front USB-C for HostLink only (the web programmer, presets and the card). It does not host a Launchpad or act as an audio interface, and it never changes the card's shared USB setting, so your other firmwares keep theirs.

Where it stands

Honest status, 2026-09-26

Done

  • Builds: 387 KB of the Lab's 480 KB program memory. The two buffers (116 KB and 64 KB) sit in fast on-chip RAM.
  • Native test suite green: all 4 modes × 4 qualities render, freeze holds the buffer, the pitch curve spans ±24 semitones.
  • That test found an upstream bug: on eurorack's master branch, Stretch mode goes silent after its first window. The one missing line is restored here and sent upstream as pichenettes/eurorack #72.

Not yet tested

  • It has not been flashed or heard on a module.
  • CPU load on the Lab's processor is unmeasured. It should be light (Clouds ran on a slower chip), but Spectral mode shares the main loop with the lights and HostLink.
  • Input level against Clouds' original ±10 V range is unverified.
  • Freeze, seed and V/Oct from real gates and CV.

Left out, compared with a real Clouds

  • Saving the audio buffer to flash (hold both buttons on the original). Presets hold the knobs only.
  • CV inputs for Texture and Blend, unless you re-route J6–J8.
  • The Parasites alternate firmware's extra modes. This is stock Clouds.

How it is built

A port, not a rewrite

The DSP is clouds::GranularProcessor from Mutable Instruments' eurorack repository at commit 08460a6, compiled unchanged apart from the one restored line. It runs at its own 32 kHz with its own 32-sample block, so the parameter smoothing reads once a millisecond exactly as the original's did. The firmware around it does what the original's cv_scaler.cc and ui.cc did: the same smoothing coefficients, the same pitch curve, the same dry/wet scaling.
