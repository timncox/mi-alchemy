What it does

The idea

Mutable Instruments' Elements is a modal synthesis voice: three exciters (a bow, a breath of noise and a strike) set a virtual object ringing, and you decide what the object is made of. This is its DSP, unchanged, running at its native 32 kHz on the Alchemy Lab.

The exciters

Bow · blow · strike

Mix them on the Exciter page (hold B3). Each has a level, a timbre and, for blow and strike, a character: FLOW turns the breath from steady to sputtering, MALLET goes from a mallet through recorded samples to bouncing particles. CONTOUR is the envelope over all three.

The resonator

What it rings in

The Play page tunes it and shapes it: GEOMETRY from plates and bars through strings to bells, BRIGHTNESS, DAMPING, and POSITION, where on the object it is excited. MODEL on the Timbre page swaps the modal resonator for a string, a string ensemble, or Ominous.

The space

Mono to reverb, and frozen

SPACE (Timbre page, hold B2) opens the two outputs from mono to stereo into Elements' reverb. The last eighth of its travel freezes the reverb, which the original module only reached with CV.

Elements had 16 knobs, 3 sliders and 7 CV attenuverters. The Alchemy Lab has six pots, so they are spread over three pages that you reach by holding a button, and the pots catch their stored values before taking over. Hold B1 to play; a gate on J3 does the same. Patch audio into J1 or J2 and it replaces the blow or strike exciter.

B2 is a page button here

In the other firmwares for this module B2 is a plain button and only B3 holds a page. Elements needs a third page, so holding B2 shows Timbre. Hold B2 and B3 together for two seconds and Settings still opens: the pager drops a layer when both are down. How that feels on the panel is one of the things to check on hardware.

Playing it

From power-up to a struck bell

  1. Patch J9 and J10 to your mixer. J9 is Elements' main output, J10 the aux; with SPACE up they are a stereo pair.

  2. Hold B1. On a first boot the voice is Elements' init sound: a struck modal resonator, STRIKE at 80%, a short CONTOUR, a little SPACE. B1 is momentary, so a tap is a short note.

  3. Tune it on the Play page. COARSE covers five octaves in semitone steps, FINE ±2 semitones. Patch 1 V/octave into J5 and a gate into J3 to play it from a sequencer.

  4. Change what it is. Sweep GEOMETRY slowly: plates, then strings, then bells and inharmonic objects. Lower BRIGHTNESS darkens the high modes, DAMPING shortens the ring, POSITION moves where it is struck.

  5. Hold B3 and bring up BOW or BLOW. Hold B2 for SPACE and MODEL. Let go and the Play page is back, each pot waiting to catch its value before it takes over.

The resonator, in your browser

An illustration computed from Elements' own code

A modal resonator is a bank of filters, one per mode of the object. Elements works out 52 of them from GEOMETRY, BRIGHTNESS and DAMPING, and weights them by POSITION. The plot below does the same arithmetic as Resonator::ComputeFilters, with the same lookup tables, and plays the result.

mode: height = level at the pickup (POSITION) tail = ring time (DAMPING, BRIGHTNESS) ·
At 0 V on J5: note = 24.1 + COARSE + 19, the firmware's formula.
Sound starts on your first press.

What is exact and what is not: mode frequencies, their count (up to 52, stopping below 0.49 of 32 kHz), stiffness from lut_stiffness, the Q from lut_4_decades and its per-mode loss are ported line for line from vendor/eurorack/elements/dsp/resonator.cc. Each mode is then played as a decaying sine (ring time Q ÷ πf, level proportional to the filter's gain) instead of Elements' state-variable filter bank, the pickup weighting uses an exact cosine where Elements uses a fast approximation, and there is no bow, no exciter envelope and no reverb. Strike is an impulse; Blow is white noise through the same response. On the module you hear the real thing.

On a first boot the Play page follows the pots, so whatever they point at is what you hear. After that, every page is saved and restored, and each pot must catch its stored value before it moves anything. The pots are shared by every firmware on the card, so after switching they point wherever the last firmware left them.

Reading the lights

What the buttons and the boot rings say
B1 · Play

The gate and the exciter

White while the gate is on. Otherwise the exciter level in orange over a dim base: dim blue when the samples are loaded, dim amber when /mi/elements.smp is missing or bad.

B2 · Timbre

The resonator

The resonator's level in green over a dim blue, Elements' second meter. Red when the audio callback averages over 80% CPU. Blue while held, as the Timbre page's colour.

B3 · Exciter

Page tint

A dim red tint at rest, the Exciter page's colour while held.

P1 ring · boot

Last session's CPU

For 2.5 s after power-up: the worst audio-block load of the previous session as a fill, green, amber from 75%, red from 90%. One dim blue pip means no data yet, after a fresh flash.

P2 ring · boot

The samples file

For the same 2.5 s: full green, loaded; one red pip, no file; full red, a file was there and was refused.

The samples live on the card

338 KB that do not fit in the firmware

Elements' strike exciter plays recorded samples and its blow exciter reads a noise recording. Together those two tables are 338 KB, and the firmware image has to fit in 480 KB of SRAM beside the SDK. So they ship as a file, and the firmware reads them into SDRAM at boot.

Where

/mi/elements.smp

A folder named mi in the root of the card, not alchemy: the firmware picker lists everything in /alchemy. The build writes the file to build-elements/elements.smp; it is 337,976 bytes.

Checked

Header, counts, CRC-32

A 24-byte header (magic MIES, version 1, the two sample counts, a CRC-32 of the payload), then 128,013 strike samples and 40,963 noise samples. Anything short of an intact file is refused. The bytes are upstream's own arrays, written by a tool that includes the vendored resources.cc.

Without it

Still plays, quieter

The two tables are zeroed: the strike samples and the blow noise go silent. The bow, the mallet synthesis, particles, the external inputs, the resonator and the reverb all still work. B1 rests dim amber and the P2 ring shows red at boot.

The file is read once, at boot, before audio starts. If the card is slow to mount, the samples stay silent until the next power-up. At build 30989c0 its SHA-256 is c1838da0e3ed431f02446bdc349998f1b8089b5139416af14eb39230424e90c5.

The gesture

Hold B2 + B3 two seconds · tap B1 to the Firmware page · B2 or B3 leaves
PotDoesRing
P1 · FILEPicks a file: one dot per .bin in /alchemy, the chosen one bright. green dots · red: no card · orange: no folder or no files
P2 · FLASHTurn it below 85%, then all the way up. The dip arms it. It compares first: if the file is the firmware already running, nothing is written. teal comparing · solid green: already loaded · orange erasing · blue writing · green verifying · white, then reboot · blinking red: failed, nothing booted
P3 · DFUSame gesture: reboots into the bootloader's update mode and stays there, for a USB flash. violet arming bar

The first Settings page holds the LED brightness (P1, 30% by default; these LEDs get very bright) and the preset gesture: P3 picks a slot, P4 saves or loads it. Elements keeps its working state in slot 9 (0-based; HostLink's list numbers from 1), saved five seconds after the last change once your hands are off the buttons. Clouds, Marbles and Plaits use 8, 10 and 11, and Smack, Mark, Belt and Relay 12–15, so slots 0–7 are yours for presets saved by hand.

Installing

From the card, or through the front USB-C
From another firmware

The picker

Copy elements_alchemy.bin into /alchemy and elements.smp into /mi, then pick it from Settings page 2 of any firmware in the family.

First time

Hold B3 at power-on

Hold B3 through the boot window and the module enters update mode. Flash the .bin with Hermetic's web programmer, or pin dfu-util to your module's serial:

make FW=elements program-live DFU_SERIAL=<serial>
Build it

From the repository

make libdaisy
make FW=elements
make -C test test-elements

Never build with a module attached in DFU mode.

Before you flash it

How it is built

The layer between Elements and the Alchemy SDK
PieceWhat it is
The DSPelements::Part from pichenettes/eurorack at 08460a6, byte-identical, at 32 kHz with its 16-frame block, so the parameter reads happen at 2 kHz as they did on the original.
The shimWhat cv_scaler.cc, ui.cc and elements.cc did: the pot laws, one-pole smoothing (0.01 for pots, 0.05 where a CV joins), the input noise gates, and the gate read one block late.
Samples out of the imageThe build cuts the two sample arrays out of a copy of resources.cc and checks it found both; the vendored file stays untouched. The engine links to two SDRAM arrays under its own symbol names, filled from the card at boot.
MemoryImage 366 KB, 77% of the 480 KB SRAM region. Part (113 KB) and the reverb buffer (64 KB) in cached D2 SRAM; the samples in SDRAM.
Per-unit characterElements seeded its reverb and resonator modulation from the chip's serial number. This uses the H750's unique ID for the same job, so each module sounds slightly its own.
Pitchnote = 24.1 + 12 × V(J5) + COARSE + 19 + FINE. The 24.1 is upstream's default calibration at 0 V, worked out from its constants: 0 V with COARSE at noon is note 73.1, about 558 Hz. Nobody has checked it by ear yet.
TestsOn a laptop, with address and undefined-behaviour checks: the samples file through the firmware's own reader in five chunk sizes, byte-identical, and six kinds of bad file refused; modal strike, bow and blow, string, strings and Ominous for 2 s each, finite and not silent; the string at note 57 reads 220.17 Hz (+1.4 cents), note 69 441.14 Hz (+4.5 cents).

Left out, compared with a real Elements

  • Elements' own V/Oct calibration: the Lab's calibrated jack voltages stand in, with upstream's default offset.
  • Five of the seven CV attenuverters and their jacks; J7 and J8 go through the SDK's CV matrix.
  • Bypass mode and the factory test port. The easter-egg boot flag is replaced by MODEL.
  • Launchpad control and USB audio: v0.1 of these ports is HostLink only, and it never changes the card's shared USB setting.

Found something? Open an issue. A report from someone playing it is worth more than anything measurable on a laptop.
